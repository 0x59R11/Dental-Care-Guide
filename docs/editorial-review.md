# Editorial review

This is a personal educational guide for teenagers and adults, primarily about permanent teeth. It is not a diagnosis tool, a pediatric dosing guide, or a certified course. The owner reviews the material and translations; professional sign-off is not a publication requirement and must not be implied.

Source links and the passages supporting the claim table were checked on **2026-11-14**. This date records source checking, not completion of the owner review below. Source display titles are translated descriptions; the linked publications retain their original language. Content and diagrams are original explanations rather than reproductions of source material.

## Diagram checks

- [ ] The tooth cross-section places enamel on the crown, dentin beneath it, pulp centrally, and roots below the gum line. Enamel is not shown as covering roots.
- [ ] Anatomy labels and accompanying text distinguish protective tissue from living pulp and root support.
- [ ] The cavity sequence includes plaque bacteria and carbohydrate metabolism before acid-related mineral loss.
- [ ] Habit comparisons use the same amount of drink when isolating consumption frequency; no invented pH measurements or risk percentages appear.
- [ ] The mineral diagram distinguishes early mineral exchange from replacing missing tooth structure.
- [ ] Progression includes the possibility of early stabilization and does not imply a fixed timeline or inevitable infection.
- [ ] Prevention links each action to a relevant mechanism; interdental cleaning includes gum health.
- [ ] Colors are legible in both themes and never carry the only explanation. Text labels and static descriptions convey the same concepts.
- [ ] Every localized diagram fits a narrow mobile viewport without abbreviating away essential meaning.

## Maintenance

1. Review sources at least annually and sooner when a cited guideline changes, a broken link is reported, or a substantive correction is proposed. The next scheduled source check is **2027-11-14**.
2. Open the actual source, confirm its current scope and publication/update date, and check whether a replacement or newer systematic review exists. The cited German S3 guideline is dated 2025-01-28 and states validity through 2030-01-27; this does not eliminate the need to check for updates.
3. Update the claim table and English copy together. Record significant evidence changes in the pull request, including whether practical advice or quiz correctness changes.
4. Update all four translations, diagram labels, and related quiz explanations in the same change. Keep stable content IDs unless the underlying lesson or question is replaced.
5. Re-run content validation and browser checks. If quiz meaning or correct answers change, increment the quiz version so stored attempts do not apply to different questions.
6. Have the owner recheck affected text and record what was reviewed and when. Do not change a source-check date into a claim of clinical review.

Unsupported claims are excluded rather than presented as alternatives of equal evidential standing. In particular, do not claim that vitamins restore structural cavities, that sugar is unrelated to caries, or that hard food guarantees jaw development.

## Content expansion — 2026-11-15

The revised content separates structural severity (including cavitation) from lesion activity. Stabilization describes little or no detectable change over an observation period; it does not imply zero mineral exchange. Arrest, remineralization, and restoration are not interchangeable. Dentin involvement can occur beneath an apparently intact surface, so the illustrated depth sequence is one possible course, not a required clinical order.
