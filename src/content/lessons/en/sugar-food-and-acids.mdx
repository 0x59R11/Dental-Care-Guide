## Sugar gives bacteria fuel

Plaque bacteria use sugar to make acids that can weaken teeth. Having less sugary food and drink helps reduce this risk.

**Both the amount and how often you have it matter.** Sipping a sweet drink for hours keeps giving bacteria more sugar. Having it with a meal avoids prolonged sipping, but does not make a large amount of sugar harmless.

## A sugar-free drink can still be acidic

Some drinks already contain acids, including many soft drinks and fruit juices. These acids can wear away the tooth surface directly. This is called **erosion**.

A sugary, acidic drink can affect teeth in two ways: bacteria use its sugar to make acid, and the drink brings its own acid. Removing the sugar does not remove the acids already in the drink.

## Small changes you can make

Choose water often. Limit sugary foods and drinks, and avoid holding acidic drinks in your mouth or sipping them for long periods.

You do not need a strict meal timetable. Look for changes that fit your day and keep your meals varied. Stomach acid from reflux or repeated vomiting can also affect teeth; discuss recurring problems with a clinician.
