## Start with plaque

**Plaque** is a sticky layer of bacteria that builds up on teeth. It is not just leftover food. Rinsing with water does not remove it as well as brushing and cleaning between teeth.

Some bacteria use sugar from food and drinks and make **acids**. These acids can weaken enamel, the hard outer covering of a tooth.

**Plaque bacteria + sugar → acids that can weaken teeth.**

## Damage can start before a hole

Repeated acid attacks can remove minerals that keep enamel strong. At first, the surface may still be whole and the tooth may not hurt.

Daily cleaning, fluoride toothpaste, and less sugar can help early damage stop getting worse or regain minerals. If damage continues, a **cavity**, or hole, may form. A hole cannot grow back; a dentist needs to assess it.

## Why cleaning still matters

Plaque can stay on your teeth between meals. Eating fewer sweets does not remove it. It can also irritate your gums, making them red, swollen, or likely to bleed.

Keep brushing twice a day and cleaning between teeth. One snack does not automatically cause a cavity: daily habits, saliva, and dental care all matter.

<details>
<summary>A little more: what happens between meals?</summary>

Bacteria do not simply switch off when you stop eating. Some can use stored carbohydrates for energy. Acid production varies; it does not always stay at the same level as during frequent sugary sipping.

Saliva helps make acids less harmful and supplies minerals. Scientists call plaque a **biofilm**, and the loss of tooth minerals **demineralization**. You do not need these terms to follow the daily care advice.

</details>
