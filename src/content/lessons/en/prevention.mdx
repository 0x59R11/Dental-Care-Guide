Prevention works by changing several parts of the process you have just learned. No single product can do every job.

## Clean and protect

**Brush twice a day for about two minutes with fluoride toothpaste.** Use gentle pressure and clean the outside, inside, and chewing surfaces, including the gumline. Spit out excess toothpaste afterward rather than immediately rinsing it all away with water.

**Clean between teeth daily.** Floss can suit tight spaces; correctly sized interdental brushes may suit larger spaces. Do not force a tool through. A dental professional can help you choose and use one comfortably.

Plaque also affects gums. It can cause inflammation, with redness, swelling, or bleeding. Cleaning helps manage plaque, but ongoing bleeding or other changes deserve assessment. Gum disease is not the same process as a cavity, even though daily cleaning matters for both.

## Food, drink, and preventive care

**Reduce sugary and acidic exposures.** Choose water often, limit sugary foods and drinks, and avoid sipping acidic drinks for long periods. Protect saliva by seeking advice about persistent dry mouth.

**Arrange preventive dental care according to your needs.** Your dentist can assess risk, notice changes, and advise on examination intervals and extra protection. There is no universal schedule for everyone.

## Keep the bigger picture in mind

Even with little sugar in the diet, plaque control remains important for teeth and gums. An established cavity can trap plaque where cleaning is difficult; prevention supports care but cannot regrow missing tissue. Ask for assessment rather than judging success from color or lack of pain.

Next, turn these ideas into a practical morning, daytime, and evening routine that you can maintain.
