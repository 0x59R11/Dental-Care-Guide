## What does a dark spot mean?

A dark spot might be a stain or a sign of decay. **Color alone cannot tell you**, and no pain does not always mean a tooth is healthy.

Arrange a dental check for a new or changing mark. Do not scrape or poke it to find out what it is.

## Damage does not always keep getting worse

Early damage may improve or stop getting worse with daily cleaning, fluoride, less sugar, and appropriate dental care. A mark can remain even after the damage has stopped.

If a weakened area has stayed the same at recent checks, keep up daily care and the checks agreed with your dentist. Improvement is not a promise that the tooth will never have problems again.

## What if there is already a hole?

A hole means some tooth material is missing. Toothpaste cannot grow it back. Keep protecting your teeth and ask a dentist what care is needed; not every damaged area needs the same treatment.

Damage can reach deeper layers and the soft center, where the nerves and blood vessels are. This can lead to infection. It does not happen on a fixed timetable, and home care alone cannot treat deep damage or infection.

<Diagram locale="en" diagramId="lesion-depth" />

## When to get help

Get new marks, suspected holes, or recurring discomfort checked rather than waiting for pain. Persistent pain or swelling needs prompt dental advice. A suspected abscess, a collection of pus from infection, needs urgent dental care.

**Swelling with difficulty breathing or swallowing needs emergency medical help.**

<summary>A little more: what do the dental terms mean?</summary>

A damaged area may be called a **lesion**. **Progression** means it is getting worse. **Stability** means little or no change has been seen over time. **Arrest** means decay has stopped progressing for now.

These do not all mean complete repair. Minerals may still move in and out of a stable area. **Remineralization** means gaining minerals; it does not rebuild missing tooth shape. A dentist considers how deep the damage is, whether it is changing, and how well it can be cleaned when advising treatment.

Damage can exist beneath an unbroken surface. A stopped area may have a hole or no hole, and its color does not prove it has stopped. The drawings show possibilities, not a way to diagnose your own tooth.

</details>
